What I do

↑

I automate everything that costs people hours and days online, so they get that time back in real life. Right now I am building comm:ntator, a service that analyses the news, helping you keep your finger on the pulse and develop your own ideas.

People have little time, and the screen takes it a minute at a time without anyone noticing: find, compare, check, miss nothing. By evening the minutes add up to hours. That work can be handed to a machine. I build services that do it on their own, bring back the finished result and leave the decision to the person.

I measure success simply: the less time a person spends in my service, the better the service is built. The hours this frees up go to places without a screen, and the mind is calmer for it.

I find where time is being wasted, design the fix, write the code with an AI assistant, launch it and count how many hours came back.

I research how to do all of this at minimal, controlled cost: for each step I pick the best technology the step really needs, no more, and I update it as new tools appear and mature.

I do not hire a team: with technology that changes the world every three months, hiring one makes no sense.

Now

↑

comm:ntator

A service for people who have something to say about the news and no time to follow it. It reads the sources, picks the stories in the author's field and drafts a post in the author's style. The author adds a point of view and publishes. I am building it so that one story takes the author five minutes.

The first working version went live three days after I started, in February 2026. Today more than 12,700 tests check the code.

Background

↑

  1. 2009

    I left advertising and started finding cheap routes for travellers, for a fixed fee per ticket. I handled the first ten requests for free and nobody bought anything: people took the route and left. So I asked for 100 roubles up front, and the orders came. That was the start of Lovibilet.

  2. 2013

    By the end of the year the Lovibilet community had 34,000 members, and I had spent nothing on advertising. The same year I launched a site where you could describe a trip in your own words. People often did not know their dates or destination, but they knew what they wanted. I wrote the program that parsed those descriptions myself. ChatGPT was nine years away.

  3. 2014

    Lovibilet made a profit every year, and 42% of customers bought again within twelve months. At the end of the year I closed it: the market had to be developed, clicks cost too much, and I did not have the money for it.

  4. 2013–2022

    Alongside Lovibilet and after it closed, I ran research at the agency Bianki & Partners: more than 800,000 respondents, field teams of up to 200 people, up to nine provinces and regions at once. In the winter of 2019–2020, on a temporary contract, I ran a study for Intel: 60 expert interviews, 10 focus groups and a survey of 500.

  5. 2022

    I moved to Spain. The next three years went into building a life from scratch: learning the language, looking for new work, meeting people.

  6. 2025

    I build AI products. Any AI model now understands a description in plain words. Finding out what a person wants is still my job.

Master's degree, ITMO University, 2007. Russian, English, Spanish. Outside work I compose music and film with a drone.
